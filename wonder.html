Seven Wonder of World

1.Taj mahal

The Taj Mahal is a famous white marble mausoleum located on the right bank of the Yamuna River in Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India. It was built by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan in memory of his beloved wife, Mumtaz Mahal, and is globally celebrated as a stunning symbol of eternal love.

Key Facts About the Taj Mahal :--

  • Location: Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India, on the Yamuna River bank.
  • Builder: Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan.
  • Time Period: Built between 1631 and 1653.
  • Material: Pure white marble inlaid with precious and semi-precious stones.

History and Construction :-

  • Shah Jahan built the tomb for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, who died in 1631 during childbirth.
  • Construction took over 20,000 artisans, craftsmen, and builders more than 20 years to complete.
  • The entire complex includes the main white dome mausoleum, formal gardens, a red sandstone mosque, and a guest house.

2.The Great Wall of China

The Great Wall of China is the world's longest man-made structure, stretching across northern China. It was built across historical northern borders to protect the Chinese states and empires against various nomadic groups from the Eurasian Steppe.

Key Facts About the Great Wall :-

  • Location: Northern China.
  • Length: 21,196 kilometres (13,171 miles) total, including all branches and sections.
  • Time Period: Built over 2,000 years, from the 7th century BC to the Ming Dynasty (1368–1644).
  • Material: Stone, brick, tamped earth, wood, and other local materials.

History and Purpose :-

  • First Emperor: Qin Shi Huang unified China around 221 BC and joined earlier walls into a single defense system.
  • Ming Dynasty: The most famous, stone-faced sections standing today were built during this era.
  • Functions: Served as border controls, a system for trade duties, a transport corridor, and a signaling system using smoke and fire.

3.Petra

The Petra is a famous archaeological site in Jordan's southwestern desert, dating back to around 300 BC. It was the capital of the Nabataean Kingdom and is world-renowned for its rock-cut architecture and innovative water conduit system.

Key Facts About Petra :-

  • Location: Ma'an Governorate, Jordan.
  • Nickname: The Rose City due to the color of the stone from which it is carved.
  • Builder: The Nabataeans, an ancient Arab people.

History and Architecture :-

  • Trading Hub: It was a major regional trading hub for frankincense, myrrh, and spices.
  • Water Engineering: Nabataeans built complex dam and cistern systems to control floods and store water in the desert.
  • Rediscovery: The site remained hidden to the Western world until Swiss explorer Johann Ludwig Burckhardt rediscovered it in 1812.

4.Colosseum

The Colosseum is the largest ancient amphitheatre ever built and stands in the centre of Rome, Italy. Commissioned by Emperor Vespasian around 70–72 AD, it is a masterpiece of Roman engineering and a global symbol of Rome's enduring history.

Key Facts About the Colosseum :-

  • Location: Rome, Lazio, Italy.Original.
  • Name: The Flavian Amphitheatre, named after the Flavian dynasty of emperors.
  • Capacity: Held between 50,000 and 80,000 spectators.
  • Status: A UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the New Seven Wonders of the World.
  • Material: Built of travertine limestone, tuff (volcanic rock), and brick-faced concrete.

History and Purpose :-

  • Entertainment: Hosted gladiatorial combats, wild animal hunts, mock sea battles, and public executions.
  • Engineering: Features the Hypogeum, an intricate underground network of tunnels and cages used to hold animals and gladiators before they were hoisted into the arena.
  • Decline: Closed for events after the fall of the Western Roman Empire and was later damaged by severe earthquakes and stone-robbers.

5.Chichén Itzá

Chichén Itzá is a world-famous pre-Columbian city built by the Maya civilization, located in Mexico’s Yucatán Peninsula. It is designated as a UNESCO World Heritage site and voted one of the New Seven Wonders of the World. At its peak between 600 and 1200 CE, it served as a powerful regional capital and a massive religious, political, and commercial trading hub.

Key Facts About Chichen :-

  • ItzaLocation: Yucatán State, Mexico.
  • Builder: The Maya civilization, with later Toltec architectural influences.
  • Peak Era: Flourished from roughly 600 AD to the 1200s AD.
  • Status: A UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the New Seven Wonders of the World.
  • Key Feature: El Castillo (The Temple of Kukulcan), a massive step-pyramid dominating the site.

History and Wonders :-

  • The Equinox Phenomenon: During the spring and autumn equinoxes, the afternoon sun creates a shadow on El Castillo that looks like a feathered serpent slithering down the pyramid staircase.
  • Advanced Astronomy: The buildings are precisely aligned with astronomical events, acting as a physical calendar for the Maya.
  • Great Ball Court: It features the largest ancient sports court in the Americas, measuring 168 by 70 metres.
  • Sacred Cenote: A natural limestone sinkhole used by the Maya for water access and religious sacrifices.

6.Machu Picchu

Machu Picchu is a 15th-century Inca citadel located in the Eastern Cordillera of southern Peru. Built at an elevation of 2,430 metres (7,970 feet) above sea level, it is the most famous icon of the Inca Empire and a masterpiece of ancient engineering.

Key Facts About Machu Picchu :-

  • Location: Cusco Region, Urubamba Province, Peru.
  • Builder: Inca Emperor Pachacuti around 1450 AD.
  • Nickname: Often called the "Lost City of the Incas."
  • Status: A UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the New Seven Wonders of the World.
  • Key Feature: Intihuatana stone, an ancient ritual stone associated with the Inca astronomic calendar.

History and Architecture :-

  • The Abandonment: It was abandoned around 100 years after its construction, during the time of the Spanish Conquest, keeping it hidden from the Spanish conquistadors.
  • Ashlar Masonry: Built using the classic Inca style of dry-stone walls. Granite blocks were cut precisely to fit together tightly without any mortar.
  • Scientific Rediscovery: Hiram Bingham, an American historian, brought the site to international attention in 1911 with the help of local indigenous farmers.
  • Agricultural Terraces: Features hundreds of man-made stone terraces used for farming, which also provided structural support against landslides and heavy rainfall.

7.Christ the Redeemer

Christ the Redeemer(Portuguese: Cristo Redentor) is an Art Deco statue of Jesus Christ located in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. Standing atop the 700-metre (2,300-foot) Corcovado mountain, it overlooks the entire city and is a global symbol of Christianity and Brazilian culture.

Key Facts About Christ the Redeemer :-

  • Location: Tijuca National Park, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.
  • Height: 30 metres (98 feet) tall, excluding its 8-metre (26-foot) pedestal. The arm span is 28 metres (92 feet).
  • Time Period: Constructed between 1922 and 1931.
  • Materials: Made of reinforced concrete and covered in a mosaic of thousands of triangular soapstone tiles.
  • Status: A UNESCO World Heritage Site (as part of Rio's cultural landscape) and one of the New Seven Wonders of the World.

History and Engineering :-

  • The Creators: Designed by Brazilian engineer Heitor da Silva Costa and sculpted by French sculptor Paul Landowski, with the face created by Romanian sculptor Gheorghe Leonida.
  • Symbolism: The statue shows Jesus with outstretched arms, representing a gesture of peace and a welcoming embrace for all.
  • Construction Challenges: All building materials and workers were transported up the steep mountain via a cog railway. It was built in sections and assembled at the summit.
  • Weather Hazards: Because it sits atop a high peak, the statue is frequently struck by lightning and features built-in lightning rods to protect the structure.
